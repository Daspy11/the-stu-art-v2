# SKY SAGA — World Bible (draft 1)

## 0. The one-line pitch

A hundred and forty years ago the ground gave way and there was nothing under it.
What stayed up is whatever people **kept coming back to**. Nobody knows why. Everyone depends on it.
You are an apprentice sexton in a town of forty-one doorsteps, and your job is to keep it in the sky by walking around and saying good morning to things.

Identity in three materials: **slate, beeswax, rope.** No brass, no goggles, no gears, no elves, no chosen ones.
Identity in one verb: **to mind** (as in "mind the step").

---

## 1. What happened

- **The Floor** — what people call the old world. "Back on the Floor." "Floor-made." Nobody alive saw it.
- **The Give** — cracks opened everywhere at once and widened over a season. Then the ground *gave*. It did not fall into anything. It fell, and behind it was pale sky, getting paler, with no bottom.
- **The Pour** — the seas ran off the edges of what was left. It took eleven years to finish. The last waterfalls are in living family memory as stories.
- **The Long Way** — what is below. It is not dark. It is *bright*: a white-gold haze that light seems to come up through. On a clear day you can see cloud a mile below you, and cloud a mile below that, and then just pale. Nothing dropped has ever been heard to land.
- Gravity goes one way. There is no "space"; the sun crosses overhead as it always did and nobody can say what it is fixed to.

### The taboo
"Down" is a swear word. So is "low". Polite people say **"away"** ("her husband went away"). Children say "what the *down*" for the thrill. Houses have no windows facing below; floors are double-planked not for strength but so there are no gaps to see through. Looking over the edge is called **looking low** and is what you are told not to do, in the way you're told not to stare at the sun.

## 2. Stay (the magic)

Some matter doesn't fall. This property is called **stay**. It is treated as a fact of nature like magnetism: measurable, tradeable, taxed, and not understood.

### What everybody knows — the rhyme

> Old holds.
> Used holds.
> Moved lets go.
> Parted pulls —
> *and don't look low.*

1. **Old holds.** Things that have been in one place a long time have stay.
2. **Used holds.** Things that are handled, walked on, *returned to* have more stay than things left alone. A doorstep beats a boulder. A well beats a wall.
3. **Moved lets go.** Take a kept thing away from its place and its people and its stay drains — slowly, over months and years.
4. **Parted pulls.** Split a kept thing in two and the halves pull toward each other, along the straight line between them, at any distance anyone has tested. (A bell and its clapper. A doorstep and a chip off it.)

Stay is counted in **years** ("a sixty-year stone"), which is how much it holds, not how old it is. It is measured with a **plumb**: a small bob of spent slate on a waxed thread. Near strong stay the thread goes slack and the bob drifts.

### What the trades know

- **Kept things** — anything with useful stay: hearthstones, doorsteps, pews, wells, bells, cradles, milestones, gravestones. *Floor-things* (pre-Give objects) are the richest and they are a finite inheritance being used up.
- **Wax takes stay; iron doesn't.** Old hive-wax, loft-timber from pigeon cotes, wool from a flock that's walked the same terrace for generations — all hold well. Nobody can say why bees and pigeons matter. (They are the two animals everyone keeps. They are also the two animals that *come home*.)
- **Minding** — the practical craft of keeping stay topped up: visiting, touching, using, greeting. It is boring and it is the most important job there is. The person in charge of it in a town is the **sexton**, who is therefore both gravedigger and chief engineer.
- **The dead hold.** A visited grave is among the steadiest things known. Towns bury their dead in the **keel** (the underside), each under their own doorstep, slung in rope cradles. "Gran went under the step. She's holding up the bakery."
- **Homers** — a flier takes a chip off their own doorstep on a thread. *Parted pulls*: it points home. It is the only compass.
- **Leaning stones** — and the doorstep, hung in the keel, leans very slightly toward its chip. A family can see which way their flier is. A stone that stops leaning and hangs straight is said to be **hanging true**. That is how you learn someone fell.
- **Kedging** — how towns move. A lead-runner flies the clapper of the town bell a few miles ahead and pegs it to a kite-anchor; the bell pulls toward its clapper and the town is drawn after. Slow. Walking pace on a good day. Sails and tow-kites help.
- **Rendering** — the industrial breakthrough (c. 80 years ago). Ring a kept thing hard enough in a wax bath and its stay goes into the wax. **Cake** (rendered wax) is portable, stackable, burnable lift. Rendering is lossy — you get perhaps a third — and the source is left **spent**: it falls like anything else.
  - Spent things are *wrong* in a way people don't like to discuss. A family walks into their rendered house and it's like a word you've said too many times. You know it's your door. It doesn't feel like your door.

### What the scholars argue

- The **College of Weights** holds that *stay is age* — a slow mineral ripening. It's respectable and it fits rule 1.
- The heresy is that *stay is use.* It fits rule 2 and explains wax, pigeons and graves.
- Anomalies neither side likes: a new cradle found holding like a century stone. An ancient untouched standing stone that dropped out of the sky one Tuesday. The fact that **the counting-house never sinks** and neither does the gaol.
- Nobody says the third thing out loud because it would be embarrassing.

### Designer truth (never stated in game; everything above must be consistent with it)

Stay is **returning**. Matter resists the fall in proportion to how much living things come back to it. Not love — *habit*. A hated tax office that everyone must visit every quarter-day holds beautifully. The Floor did not have "ground" in the way people imagine: it was *kept*, by something that kept coming back to it, and one season that something stopped. What it was is the long mystery of the saga.

Second designer truth: **the Long Way is a loop.** Far enough down is up. Unkept matter takes centuries to come round — the Floor itself is still on its way, and the first of it is due within a lifetime. But *parted pulls* along the shortest line: for the first half of the fall the pull is back upward (kept things really do fall slower — a folk belief nobody has proved); past the halfway point the shortest line to home runs *onward*, and the pull speeds the fall. A strongly paired thing can come round in a couple of years. On the day it crosses halfway, its leaning stone — which has hung true, pulled straight down — swings to pull **straight up**. No one has ever seen this. Chapter One starts on the morning one does.

## 3. How anyone is alive

Life is hard and the answers are all partial.

- **Air.** Nobody knows why the air didn't go with the Pour. It is the first question in every natural philosophy lecture and it is not on the exam. (Truth: everything alive returns to it with every breath.) Old people insist it's thinner than when they were young.
- **Water.** There are no rivers and no sea. Water is **combed**: fog-nets of spider silk rigged on spars catch cloud and drip into slate cisterns. Towns follow weather the way herds follow grass; this is the main reason towns move at all. Water is rationed by the cup. People wash with ash and sand. A wet week is a holiday.
- **Height is climate, and climate is class.**
  - **The Bright** (high): cold, dry, thin, sunny. Good grain, clean lungs, no water. Where the rich cities sit. Staying there costs stay.
  - **The Lanes** (middle): where most towns drift and trade. Weather comes through here.
  - **The Damps** (low): warm, wet, fog all day, everything grows and everything rots. Damp-lung. Close to the pale. Towns that are losing stay end up here. They grow the food.
  - Below the Damps is the **Last Light** line, where the haze is bright enough that you cast a shadow *upward*. A town seen below Last Light is struck from the registers.
  - So: water and food go **up**; cake, law and assessors come **down**.
- **Food.** Terraces on top, net-bags of soil hung down the flanks (soil is wealth: "dirt-rich"), mushrooms in the keel, goats, bees, and above all pigeons — meat, eggs, post, and guano, which is the only fertiliser. Lichen-flour bread in bad years. Nobody is fat.
- **Warmth.** No ground means no shelter from wind. Everything is felted. Houses are low, round-shouldered and tarred, with slate skirts. Fuel is goat dung, pruned wood and — in rich cities — cake, which poor people consider obscene, like burning a church.
- **Rope and silk.** Each autumn ballooning spiders rise out of the Damps in their millions — **the Silking**. Towns net it. It's where all rope, thread and fog-net comes from. The riggers' guild is called **the Ropewalk**.
- **Wild islands sink.** A rock nobody lives on loses a few feet a year. Nobody talks about what this means for the long run.

## 4. How people are organised (small to large)

- **A holding** — a household and its kept things: hearthstone, doorstep, cradle. When you move house you carry the step. Marriage is "sharing a step".
- **A town** (a few hundred) — one island with a built keel. Officers:
  - the **Sexton** minds the stay (and the dead);
  - the **Tally** keeps **the Ledger**, the public account of the town's stay, hearth by hearth. A town *in the black* holds its height. A town *in the red* is sinking;
  - the **Loft** keeps the pigeons, which are the post, and therefore the news.
- **A drift** — a loose fleet of towns that follow the same weather and marry each other's children.
- **The climber cities** — a handful of great stacked cities in the Bright, tens of thousands each, kept up by rendering. They cannot make stay as fast as they burn it. So they send **assay vessels** down the Lanes to inspect small towns' ledgers. A town found in the red is declared *sinking* and **relieved** of its kept stones "for the safety of its people", who are resettled in the city's keel.
  - City keels are full of **wearers**: people paid by the hour to walk the same stairs, sit the same benches, open the same doors. Manufactured habit. It works, a bit.
  - The cities are not villains in their own eyes. Their arithmetic is correct: small towns *do* sink, and the stones *would* be lost.
- **The Lofts** — pigeon-keepers everywhere answer to each other before they answer to any town. The only institution that spans the sky. Gossip is infrastructure.

---

## 5. Tone

Play and gravity in the same breath, never winking. People in this world are funny because they are practical about an unbearable situation. The pigeon is a civil servant. The bathtub flies a bit. The dead are colleagues. Nobody makes a speech.

The horror is quiet and it is *down*. It is bright, patient and has no monsters in it.

## 6. Chapter One cast (Latchford — forty-one doorsteps, low in the Lanes, drifting toward the Damps)

"Ford", "bridge", "port" survive in place names. Nobody knows what a ford was.

- **Mote** (player, ~13). Apprentice sexton. Wears a wax-yellow flier's coat four sizes too big. It was their mother's spare. Says little; the player's choices are small and dry.
- **Sexton Abel Thrum.** Enormous, round, slow. Bottle-green felt. Wears a small bell on his belt "so they hear me coming — it's only polite." Has never been heard to raise his voice and has never needed to.
- **Deputy.** A pigeon. Officially Deputy Postmaster. Lives on Thrum's hat. Regards Mote as a junior colleague.
- **Mrs. Quill, the Tally.** Tall, narrow, ink-blue. Counts everything, including your footsteps. Terrified, and deals with it by being exact.
- **Brick.** Eleven. Red scarf. Named, unkindly, after a thing with no stay. Is building a skiff out of his family's bathtub — which, having had three generations of Saturday nights, genuinely hovers about a hand off the deck. Says "down" as often as possible.
- **Ansel Mote's mother — "Pilot Marrin".** Latchford's lead-runner. Went out with the clapper two years ago in clear weather. Her stone has hung true since. Never appears. Her doorstep is the third stone on the keel-walk and it has a chip out of one corner.
